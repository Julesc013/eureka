# Project Identity and Purpose

The corpus converges on Eureka as a local-first, evidence-backed, temporal artefact-resolution system. Search is the visible entry point, but the product-bearing idea is resolution: identify an object, explain its evidence state, preserve uncertainty, and turn unresolved searches into durable future work.

## Key synthesis

The strongest decision is to reject answer-first search. Eureka should not be a Google clone, an Archive.org wrapper, a generic crawler, an app store, or an LLM answer surface. It should expose verified artifacts, candidates, needs, near misses, bounded absences, and review state.

## Unresolved questions

The largest identity question is breadth: whether the first product story should remain old software/archive resolution or explicitly broaden now into all provenance-obscured technical/cultural artefacts through domain packs.

## Contradictions or drift

The drift is mostly productive: earlier “search engine” language was superseded by “evidence-first resolver” and later by “universal artefact-resolution engine.” The older term can remain a user-facing metaphor, not the architecture.

## Implications for future work

Future work should keep the public UX search-first while making the backend investigation-first. The first practical proof is a query that becomes reviewed results, candidates, SearchNeeds, WorkUnits, evidence, review, and index refresh.
